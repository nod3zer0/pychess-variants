# Lao Tzu

Lao Tzu Chess is a dark chess variant invented by John Kipling Lewis in 2005, shortly after Sun Tzu Chess, as a refinement of its fog of war. It combines Fog of War, Crazyhouse-style drops, and Double Fischer Random Chess, exactly like Sun Tzu Chess, with one key difference in the drop rule.

## Rules

- **Fog of War**: a player does not see the entire board – only their own pieces and the squares that they can legally move to. As in Fog of War chess, there are no checks; the game ends when a king is captured.
- **Drops**: captured pieces go to your hand and can be dropped back onto the board, as in Crazyhouse – but, unlike Sun Tzu Chess, a drop is only allowed on a square you can already see through your pieces' ordinary moves. This keeps far more of the board hidden for far longer, since dropping a piece no longer reveals empty squares the way it does in Sun Tzu Chess.
- **Double Fischer Random setup**: pawns start on their usual squares, but the remaining pieces are placed randomly on the back rank, with the king between the two rooks and the bishops on opposite-colored squares – independently for each side, so White's and Black's back ranks are not mirror images of each other.

For the original variant where drops are allowed on any empty square, see Sun Tzu.
